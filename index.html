The explainer

Why can a few marks change thousands of ranks?

Because rank is not a property of your score alone. It is your position inside one year’s entire score distribution.

01

The queue

Marks tell us what you scored. Rank tells us how many people are ahead.

If a dense cluster of candidates lands near the same score, the curve gets steep: one mark crosses more people. At 508, this model currently crosses about 244 candidates per mark.

The shorter paper

180 questions changes the texture of the score curve.

Previous papers had 200 questions. With 10% fewer items, each question carries more of the percentage score. Under a simple independent-item approximation, score variability is about 5.4% wider—one reason percentage-only scaling is not enough.

Inspect the method ↘

Method & sources

Transparent enough to disagree with.

This is an independent pre-result model, not an NBEMS tool. Its assumptions are visible so you can use it as a planning aid—not a promise.

How the weighted centre is made

The model simulates a calibrated recent upper-tail distribution, then evaluates four 180-question outcomes: 45% recent baseline, 25% harder paper, 15% mixed response and 15% easier paper. The weighted AIR is their planning centre, not a known result.

What the uncertainty band means

The lower AIR edge is the harder-paper scenario; the upper AIR edge is the easier-paper scenario. It is a scenario envelope, not a statistical confidence interval. The upper edge is useful for conservative planning, but it is not a guaranteed worst case.

Why 508/720 is not simply 564/800

That arithmetic preserves percentage only. It does not preserve difficulty, variance, clustering or cohort size. The 2026 format has 180 questions in five locked sections of 36 questions and 42 minutes each; fewer items also make each raw mark a larger share of the paper.

What changes after the result

Once official 2026 score–rank observations are available, this simulation should be replaced—not invisibly blended with the real curve. Until then, do not use it as the sole basis for a specialty, college or counselling decision.

How to use RankLab

  1. 1
    Set or calculate your marks

    Drag, type, use ±5, or open “Calculate from answers” to score your correct, wrong and unattempted questions.

  2. 2
    Read the centre and the band

    The weighted AIR is the default planning point. The harder-to-easier scenario band matters more before results.

  3. 3
    Compare scenarios

    Harder is favourable at a fixed mark; easier is adverse. Recent and mixed show the two middle model shapes.

  4. 4
    Plan, don’t conclude

    Use the easier-paper edge for conservative shortlisting. Replace every estimate with your official AIR when released.
